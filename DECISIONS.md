# Slice 1 decisions

- A viewer is rate limited independently within their community: one guess every 3,000 ms. A rejected or unclear guess also consumes that interval, which prevents rapid brute force.
- A correct guess records a community's first solver. A round becomes won on the first correct guess from the other community; its solve time is measured from start to that guess.
- Only timed-out rounds reset the streak. Pending and active rounds do not affect it; the caller records completed outcomes with `recordResult`.
- Puzzle text is normalized by removing accents and punctuation and lowercasing before containment checks. Blocklisted terms are checked as whole words.
- The referee accepts exact normalized answers/aliases or edit distance up to 1 for answers of at most 4 characters, 2 up to 8, and 3 for longer answers. Near misses at distance up to 3 beyond that accepted threshold are unclear.
- Session persistence stores puzzle, round status, timestamps, first solvers, guess counts/history, and streak as JSON.

## Slice 2a and 2b decisions

- Each puzzle carries two authored, distinct alternative-answer lists. They are the reveal promise shown to viewers; code checks their presence, uniqueness, and that they do not repeat the answer or aliases. Whether an alternative genuinely fits one half and is ruled out by the other remains an editorial check.
- The pack contains 60 authored entries, adds `halfAAlsoFits`/`halfBAlsoFits`, aliases, and easy/medium/hard labels. It includes Indian sport, food, cities, music, and films.
- `CHANNEL_A` and `CHANNEL_B` are Twitch logins. Startup resolves them, and the authenticated bot, to user IDs before creating EventSub subscriptions and sending chat.
- OAuth role state is held in memory for the short authorization round trip; access and refresh tokens are persisted to ignored `data/tokens.json`. Token refresh starts when less than 60 seconds remain.
- `!tc start` and `!tc skip` require Twitch broadcaster or moderator badges. `!tc score` and `!g` are available to all viewers. A moderator from either channel controls the shared round.
- Chat sends use one serial queue across both channels, with a one-second default gap and Twitch `Retry-After` handling for HTTP 429.
- Twitch integration is interface-driven and unit-tested with fake EventSub sockets, fake Helix, and fake HTTP token/chat responses. Real credentials are required for live Twitch operation and were not available for this slice.

## Slice 3a and 3b decisions

- Blind audits use the configured OpenAI-compatible endpoint and make three sequential requests per puzzle so half A, half B, and the pair are independent. A pass requires the referee to reject each solo best answer and accept the combined best answer. The audit stores clue-content fingerprints so edited clue pairs cannot reuse stale results.
- `AUDIT_MODEL` can override `LLM_MODEL`; the audit script falls back to `LLM_MODEL` only when the audit model returns 404. Audit output is committed under `src/puzzle/audit-report.json`, while resumable checkpoints stay in ignored `data/`.
- The configured Groq endpoint's available Qwen model was used at low concurrency after its free tier rate limited larger models. Its current blind audit passed 20 of 60 puzzles (33.3%). This is a measured failure, not a release-ready pack; the report lists every failing clue and reason in `docs/PUZZLE_AUDIT.md`.
- Overlays receive a side-specific state: only the active community's half is sent, while guess activity is sent as anonymous per-side counts. Full clues, answer, names of first solvers, and that side's also-fits list appear only at reveal. CSS transitions and the countdown run in the browser.
- The panel talks to the same in-memory controller as Twitch and the overlay. Settings constrain round length to 30–600 seconds and category/difficulty choices to the built-in pack. The local demo uses a fresh non-persistent session and scripted fake guesses, and labels the pages DEMO.
- The HTTP control panel is intended for local streamer use and currently has no login or CSRF protection; it should be bound behind a trusted local setup before public exposure.

## Slice 4 decisions

- Pack repair retained only the 32 hand-written puzzles whose current clue fingerprints passed the blind audit. Failed IDs are replaced by model-generated candidates; the referee and audit prompt remain unchanged.
- The generator asks `LLM_MODEL` for batches of up to four candidates, rejects structural violations and duplicate answers, then sends each valid candidate through the serial blind auditor. It persists candidate progress under ignored `data/` so an interrupted free-tier run can resume. Passing candidates are selected to balance categories and difficulty, with at least five India-friendly generated puzzles to bring the full pack to eight or more.
- Generation also rejects an also-fits entry that the existing referee would accept as the intended answer, which catches near-typos such as a one-letter miss.
- Overlays are compact lower thirds anchored to their community's outer edge. A completed round expands for a short celebration and reveal, then collapses. Teal and coral identify each community, and both colours meet on the shared-link animation.
- The demo runs a cooperative round with a ten-second wait between the first and second community solves, then a timeout round, with a short idle beat between rounds. Its controller is in-memory and non-persistent.
- The streamer panel embeds both live overlay pages as side-by-side previews and keeps start, skip, category, difficulty and duration controls in large hit targets.

## Slice 5 decisions

- Audit v2 keeps the blind prompt shape of one best answer plus two alternatives. A puzzle fails if any solver accepts either solo clue, needs a majority to accept the paired clue, and requires the separate fact verifier to mark both halves true. The audit fingerprint includes the audit version and model names, so v1 results cannot be reused.
- Blind requests use opaque IDs and never include the answer. Each request carries clues of only one kind and no more than one prompt per puzzle. The configured maximum is eight; the implementation caps solver batches at four because larger model responses omitted clue IDs. Fact requests can include the answer and both halves.
- Audit requests use reasoning effort medium and a 6,144-token completion cap so reasoning has room for the JSON response. If a provider rejects medium reasoning, the client retries without that parameter. Structured output falls back to JSON mode and then local JSON validation. Per-minute waits use the provider's named delay; daily quota stops preserve puzzle results and model token totals under ignored `data/` checkpoints.
- Pack generation is blocked until the old pack audit completes. It will keep only v2 passes, target 60, cap India-friendly entries at 15 while keeping at least eight, and prioritize at least 15 games puzzles. It uses `LLM_MODEL` and switches to `openai/gpt-oss-20b` if the generation model's daily quota is spent.
- Overlay state reports live, solved, time-up, and idle explicitly. The ticker shows only the side's last three guesses, with correct guesses represented as “got it” until reveal. The guess prefix comes from `COMMAND_PREFIX`, defaulting to `!g`; singular score copy uses “win.”
- During this work the free endpoint completed 8 of the 60 old puzzles before daily quota exhaustion. None of those eight passed v2. The checkpoint and token totals are retained; the remaining 52 old puzzles must be audited after the quota resets before generation can safely start.

## Slice 5b decisions

- A viewer owns one editable vote in their community. Recasting moves their previous vote. Aliases and accepted fuzzy spellings share the answer bucket; every other vote uses the whole normalized guess.
- A side's unique plurality leader is its result. Ties have no leader. The round is won only if both side leaders are correct, whether the round ends by timer or streamer lock.
- Early finish requires 15 seconds of play, correct unique leaders at 50 percent or more on both sides, and five continuous seconds in that state. A streamer lock ends voting immediately and records a win only when both leaders are correct.
- The overlay shows vote text and counts but withholds correctness until reveal. It shows only the current community's top three buckets and the other community's clue remains hidden. Recent samples are capped at 80 per round.
- The forge search is deterministic and uses only one or two typed attribute predicates per half. Each predicate set needs at least five entities, and at least two entities in each set must have fame at least equal to the answer.
- Audit v2.1 uses provider-prefixed Gemini and Groq model names. Gemini calls use its OpenAI-compatible endpoint and a separate key. The gpt-oss solver uses low reasoning. Model and audit version are included in fingerprints.
- Forge checkpoints now retain the selected candidate wording, completed puzzle results, and per-model token totals so a quota resume audits the same clues.
- The old pack remains in docs/archive/slice-5. The production game reads only the forged audited pack, including when it contains fewer than 40 puzzles. It never falls back to unaudited clues. Demo mode appends a small unaudited sample pack and marks those rounds in the overlay.

## Slice 5c decisions

- Candidate selection is one puzzle per normalized answer, globally across source tables. The canonical unordered predicate pair prevents swapped mirrors from surviving.
- The 80 puzzle target uses fixed category quotas: games 18, geography 14, animals 12, food 12, music 8, space 8, and sports 8. A quota shortfall is reported. The forge does not duplicate answers to fill it.
- Clue text comes from category-specific predicate templates. Model polishing is disabled because a prior saved candidate changed Chun-Li's Capcom predicate into Sony wording. Each half must remain within 14 words, and series or franchise names cannot appear in game clues.
- The fact checker stores one cached verdict per answer and attribute value in `facts/verdicts.json`. A false or unsure check removes the candidate. Table cleanup set Link to Hylian and Kratos to demigod, and removed Waluigi's disputed species value and the unreliable broad animal diet field.
- Provider request pacing is configurable through `AUDIT_RPM_GEMINI`, `AUDIT_RPM_GROQ`, and `AUDIT_RPM_OPENAI`. Gemini retry metadata is respected. A solver failure does not stop the remaining solver panel; if none can finish, the checkpoint is retained and the forge exits.
- The current tables produce 25 unique candidates. Provider quotas prevented a complete audit, so none are shipped yet. Gemini models used zero tokens; Groq used 1,843 tokens during partial attempts. The empty production pack remains safe, while demo mode labels sample rounds as unaudited.

## Slice 6 decisions (production reliability)

- **One bot socket.** Twitch refuses subscriptions from different users on one WebSocket session, so the bot's user token alone subscribes `channel.chat.message` (needs only `user:read:chat`) and `channel.shared_chat.begin`, `update` and `end` (need no scope) for both channels on one socket. Broadcaster tokens are never used on it. The Channel Points path (one socket per affiliate broadcaster) is not built, because the host channel is not an affiliate.
- **Least privilege.** The bot is authorised with `user:read:chat` and `user:write:chat` only. `BOT_LOGIN` defaults to `CHANNEL_A`, so the host can be the bot. Side B needs only a login name in `CHANNEL_B`; a missing `CHANNEL_B` never blocks side A.
- **OAuth.** `/auth/start` is a POST that needs the admin token and returns the Twitch URL. States are random, single use and expire after 10 minutes. On the callback the new token is checked with `/oauth2/validate` and refused unless its login matches the role's expected login. Tokens are validated at start and hourly; a 401 triggers one refresh. Concurrent refreshes share one request. `data/tokens.json` is written through a temp file with mode 0600, and an existing file is tightened to 0600 on load.
- **Admin token.** `ADMIN_TOKEN` (at least 24 characters) is required for the panel's API, `/api/*`, `/auth/start` and `/metrics`, sent as `Authorization: Bearer` or `X-Admin-Token`, compared in constant time. The panel page itself is static; it asks for the token and keeps it in session storage. Its WebSocket proves itself with a first `auth` message, since browsers cannot set socket headers, and is closed with 4401 otherwise. Requests whose Origin is another site are refused.
- **Overlay keys.** Each side's key is an HMAC of the admin token, so one secret is managed and key A never opens side B. The page and its socket both check the key, and the socket also checks Origin. Demo mode has no admin token, so it refuses `PUBLIC=1` and binds to 127.0.0.1. The server binds to 127.0.0.1 unless `PUBLIC=1`.
- **The panel never shows both halves.** Panel state carries no clue text. The panel previews only overlay A (the host's own) and offers overlay B's link as a copy button for streamer B.
- **Consensus lock replaces the early finish.** The old early finish checked that both leaders were correct, which made an early end an answer oracle. Now a round locks early when both sides have a unique leader with at least 60 percent of that side's voters, at least 3 voters each, held for 5 seconds. Correctness is computed only at the reveal. Locks and timeouts behave as before.
- **Closed vocabulary.** A round's vocabulary is every entity of the puzzle's fact table (puzzles record `sourceTable`; puzzles without one use the answer and both also-fits lists). Every entity, not only the answer, is matched the same way: exact name or alias, then the referee's edit-distance threshold, with ties left unmatched. So how a vote is grouped reveals nothing. Vote bars, the leader line and the ticker show only entity names; everything else is one "other answers" figure. The blocklist stays as a second layer. Frames never contain an answer key.
- **Chat replies.** The bot posts only round start, round result and a recap (a panel button). It no longer replies to `!tc score`, refusals or commands. Rehearsal rounds post nothing.
- **Chat limits.** One sliding window of 20 messages per 30 seconds for the bot user plus a 1 second gap per channel, which is Twitch's limit for a bot that is not a moderator or VIP (exceeding it gets the bot ignored for an hour). A 429 pauses until `Ratelimit-Reset`. `is_sent: false` is a failure and is logged with its drop reason. While Shared Chat joins both channels, a message is sent once. The queue holds at most 20 messages.
- **Identity.** Votes are keyed by `chatter_user_id`, so renames and display-name changes do not create a second vote. `commands.ts` now compares the chatter id with the broadcaster id; it used to compare a display name with an id, so the owner check never matched. Moderator status in Shared Chat comes from `source_badges`.
- **EventSub.** On `session_reconnect` the client opens the given URL, keeps the old socket until the new welcome, then closes it, and does not resubscribe. The reconnect URL is single use and a fresh session always starts from the default URL. Unexpected closes, keepalive silence (keepalive window plus 2 seconds) and a missing welcome (15 seconds) reconnect with exponential backoff from 1 second with half jitter, capped at 30 seconds and reset on welcome. Close codes 4000 to 4007 are logged with their meaning. All subscriptions are sent in parallel right after the welcome; 202 and 409 count as enabled, 401 and 403 ask for re-authorisation, every failure is recorded per subscription, and nothing can reject unhandled. Revocations mark that channel's subscription and the panel says "Re-authorise channel X". Health is tracked per side.
- **Dedupe.** Chat is deduplicated on `source_message_id ?? message_id`, so the two copies of one Shared Chat message count once, and the side comes from the source channel. A message from a third Shared Chat channel is ignored. Other notifications dedupe on the envelope message id. Notifications older than 10 minutes are dropped. The cache keeps 50,000 ids or 10 minutes, whichever is smaller. The brief suggested 10,000 ids, but at the 10,000 per second burst that is only one second of history.
- **Hot path.** Each side's tally indexes buckets by count, so a vote, a vote move and the leader lookup are O(1). Vote text is capped at 200 characters before any normalisation. The vote-change cooldown is 2 seconds (it was 3) and applies only to changing a vote; per-viewer cooldown state is cleared when the round ends. Fuzzy vocabulary lookups are cached per round (at most 50,000 entries).
- **Overlay frames.** Frames are coalesced to at most one per 100 ms per view and serialised once per view, not per socket. Round transitions flush immediately and are never skipped. A socket with more than 512 KB buffered skips a frame and gets the latest one 250 ms later. Above 400 votes per second the ticker keeps one sample per 50 ms; skipped samples are counted as shed. Votes and transitions are never shed. Frames carry `serverNow` so a remote overlay's countdown corrects for clock skew.
- **State.** The session file now holds the score, the streak, the last 50 round summaries and the current round. Votes are stored compactly as `[user id, bucket index]`, with entity buckets stored by name. Saves are atomic (temp file then rename), at most one per second while votes arrive (stretched to ten times the last write's duration when the vote map is large) and immediate on round transitions and shutdown. A corrupt file is renamed to `session.json.corrupt-<time>` with a loud error log. A resumed round keeps its stored end time; if that passed while the server was down, it finishes on start. Rehearsal rounds never change the score.
- **Observability.** `/healthz` is liveness. `/readyz` returns 503 with plain reasons per side (bot token, channel id, EventSub connection, chat subscription). `/metrics` (admin token) exports messages in, votes by outcome, duplicates by reason, shed work, reconnects by reason, chat sends by result, overlay clients, snapshots and a receive-to-frame latency histogram. Logs are Fastify's JSON logs; request URLs are logged without query strings (overlay keys, OAuth codes), and token-shaped fields are redacted. A test asserts that no secret reaches the log stream.
- **Rehearsal.** A panel toggle enables rehearsal. The host can then type votes for community B, each as a new simulated viewer. The panel and both overlays show "REHEARSAL". Real mode hides the vote box.
- **Not built in this slice.** The `!hint` vote and Channel Points path, and an automated Twitch CLI check.
- **Forge coverage.** The fact tables were enriched with well-known categorical attributes (and some entities) so every table clears its quota: `npm run forge -- --dry-run` now finds 196 unique-answer candidates (games 37, countries 65, animals 17, food 34, music 13, space 13, sports 17). A curated animal `diet` attribute is back, replacing the removed broad one. The search keeps the best valid candidate per answer, skips pairs whose second predicate adds nothing, and uses bitmasks. `--dry-run` makes no model calls and writes nothing. The paid audit and fact check have not been rerun.

## Slice 6 follow-up decisions

- **One person, one side.** Within a round, a `chatter_user_id` that has voted for one community is refused on the other (`wrong-side`, counted in `twinclue_votes_total{result="wrong_side"}`). A restored snapshot never places one viewer on both sides.
- **Minimum voters.** A round that ends with fewer than `MIN_VOTERS_TO_WIN` voters (default 2) on either side ends unscored as "not enough voters". This applies to every ending (timer, lock, consensus), because a lock with one voter per side is as thin as a timeout. The consensus lock already needs 3 voters per side, so it is unaffected at the default.
- **Skip is not scored.** A skip (panel or `!tc skip`) ends the round as unscored ("skipped"): no win, and the score, round count and streak are unchanged. Unscored rounds appear in history with their reason, the overlay shows "NOT SCORED", and the bot's result message says the round did not count.
- **Who may skip.** The safer option: only the host's panel and community A's broadcaster and moderators can skip. Community B's broadcaster and moderators get no skip, because a skip voids the round for both communities. They can still lock and start.
- **Readiness.** `/readyz` publicly returns only `{"ready": true|false}` with 200 or 503. The reasons and per-side detail need the admin token, because they name channels and accounts.
- **OAuth.** EventSub restarts run through one promise chain, so two quick callbacks cannot leave two clients running. A token from the wrong account is revoked with `POST /oauth2/revoke` before it is discarded. Token file writes are queued and use unique temp names; two concurrent sign-ins used to race on one temp file.
- **Demo mode** generates a random 48-character admin token at each start and prints the panel link containing it (after `#`, so it never reaches the server or its logs) once to stdout. The demo is therefore protected like a live server and may also bind publicly.
- **Origin allow-list.** WebSocket upgrades and admin requests that carry an Origin must match an explicit list: PUBLIC_URL plus `localhost`, `127.0.0.1` and `[::1]` on the listening port. The Host header is no longer trusted, which closes DNS rebinding. Requests without an Origin (non-browser clients) are still allowed, and still need the overlay key or the admin token.
- **Snapshot timing** is now measured: `twinclue_snapshot_blocking_seconds` (synchronous serialisation) and `twinclue_snapshot_seconds` (including the file write).
